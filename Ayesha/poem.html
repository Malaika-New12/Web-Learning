The Peace of Wild Things

                When despair for the world grows in me
              and I wake in the night at the least sound
        in fear of what my life and my children’s lives may be,
                 I go and lie down where the wood drake
     rests in his beauty on the water, and the great heron feeds.

               I come into the peace of wild things
          who do not tax their lives with forethought
     of grief. I come into the presence of still water.
           And I feel above me the day-blind stars
            waiting with their light. For a time
    I rest in the grace of the world, and am free.
Author: Wendell Berry

thought behind the poem

The poem is about finding peace when life feels overwhelming. Wendell Berry describes how worries about the future can make us anxious and afraid. Instead of allowing those thoughts to control him, he turns to nature the quiet water, birds, trees, and stars. The main message is that nature can help us forget our worries and bring peace to our minds. Sometimes, we become so focused on tomorrow that we forget to appreciate the calmness and beauty of today. The poet reminds us to slow down, connect with nature, and simply live in the present moment.

Main idea:

When life becomes difficult, sometimes the simplest things—nature, silence, and being present—can give us the peace we need.

quote

A quote that beautifully matches the poem is:

“Look deep into nature, and then you will understand everything better.”
— Albert Einstein
why it matches

The poem reminds us that when we feel worried or overwhelmed, nature can bring us peace and help us see life more clearly. This quote carries the same message: nature has the power to calm us, teach us, and reconnect us with ourselves.
